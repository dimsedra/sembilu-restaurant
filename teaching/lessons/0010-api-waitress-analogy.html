The Sembilu Waitress as the API

Lesson 10 · A real walkthrough of ordering Ikan Bakar — through the waitress, not around her.

👩‍🍳 Meet Wati. She's been a waitress at Sembilu Tegal for 5 years. She knows the menu by heart, she knows which tables are free, she knows the chef's name. When you want something, you tell Wati. Wati handles the rest.

What Wati does (and doesn't do)

"I want Ikan Bakar"Writes it down, goes to kitchenChef reads the order
"Is table 5 free?"Checks the seating chart—
"Add extra sambal"Adds a note to the orderChef sees the note
"Where's my food?"Goes to kitchen, asks chef"Almost done!"

What you DON'T do:

  • ❌ Walk into the kitchen and cook yourself
  • ❌ Open the chef's recipe book and cross things out
  • ❌ Talk to the chef directly in Javanese
  • ❌ Yell across the room to another table's customer

And what Wati DOESN'T do:

  • ❌ Decide the prices (that's the manager)
  • ❌ Cook the food (that's the chef)
  • ❌ Let you into the kitchen to check on your order

That's why the API exists. Wati (the API) is a controlled gateway. She only lets you do what you're allowed to do:

  • ✅ See the menu
  • ✅ Place an order
  • ✅ Check your order status
  • ❌ Delete menu items
  • ❌ See other customers' orders
  • ❌ Change prices

So the tickets I proposed are…

Each ticket builds a small piece of Wati's knowledge:

TicketWhat Wati learns to do
T1 — Dishes APIWhen a customer asks "what's on the menu?", Wati can answer from memory (the database)
T2 — Sambals APIWhen asked "what sambals do you have?", Wati can list all 9
T3 — Reservations APIWhen you call "can I book a table for Saturday?", Wati checks the book and pencils you in
T4 — Orders APIWhen you say "I'll have the Ikan Bakar," Wati writes the order slip and gives it to the chef
T5 — WebSocketWati gets a little earpiece — now when the chef says "order 42 is done," she hears it instantly without walking to the kitchen
T6 — Staff AuthWati starts checking ID before letting people into the staff area. Not everyone is allowed back there.
T7 — Staff OrdersWati can now tell the chef "table 5 wants extra pedas" and tell the waiter "order 42 is ready to serve"
T8 — Staff ReservationsWati can show the hostess "here's who's coming tonight"
T9 — TablesWati knows which tables are free, occupied, or reserved — no more walking around to check

By the end of 9 tickets, Wati is a fully trained waitress who can run the entire front-of-house operation. The frontend (React app) is just a fancy way for customers and staff to talk to Wati without needing to be in the restaurant.

The takeaway: API = Wati. Frontend = you at the table. Database = the kitchen. Wati is the only one who talks to the kitchen. You talk to Wati. That's the rule.
